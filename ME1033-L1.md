# 📚 Complete Study Notes: Products of Vectors

---

## 🎯 Introduction & Overview

Vectors can be "multiplied" in several different ways, each producing different results with unique geometric meanings. Understanding these products is fundamental to physics, engineering, and advanced mathematics.

### Why Do We Need Different Vector Products?

| Situation | What We Need | Which Product |
|-----------|--------------|---------------|
| Finding angle between vectors | A number (scalar) | Dot Product |
| Calculating work done by force | A number (scalar) | Dot Product |
| Finding a perpendicular direction | A new vector | Cross Product |
| Calculating torque/moment | A new vector | Cross Product |
| Finding volume of 3D shapes | A number (scalar) | Scalar Triple Product |
| Checking if points lie on same plane | Test for zero | Scalar Triple Product |

---

# 1. 🔵 The Dot Product (Scalar Product)

## What Is It?

The dot product is a way to multiply two vectors that gives a **scalar** (a single number). It measures how much one vector "goes in the same direction" as another.

> **Physical Intuition:** Think of it as measuring "alignment" between vectors. Maximum when parallel, zero when perpendicular, negative when pointing opposite directions.

---

## Definition & Formulas

### Geometric Definition
$$\Large \boxed{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\theta}$$

Where:
- $|\vec{a}|$ = magnitude of vector $\vec{a}$
- $|\vec{b}|$ = magnitude of vector $\vec{b}$  
- $\theta$ = angle between vectors $(0 \leq \theta \leq \pi)$

### Algebraic Definition (Component Form)

If $\vec{a} = a_1\hat{i} + a_2\hat{j} + a_3\hat{k}$ and $\vec{b} = b_1\hat{i} + b_2\hat{j} + b_3\hat{k}$:

$$\Large \boxed{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3}$$

> **💡 Memory Trick:** "Multiply matching components, then add everything up"

---

## Finding Angles Between Vectors

Combining both formulas gives us the **angle formula**:

$$\Large \boxed{\cos\theta = \frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|} = \frac{a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3}{\sqrt{a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2} \cdot \sqrt{b_1^2+b_2^2+b_3^2}}}$$

---

## Key Properties

| Property | Formula | Meaning |
|----------|---------|---------|
| **Commutative** | $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = \vec{b} \cdot \vec{a}$ | Order doesn't matter |
| **Distributive** | $\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} + \vec{c}) = \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + \vec{a} \cdot \vec{c}$ | Can expand brackets |
| **Scalar Multiple** | $(k\vec{a}) \cdot \vec{b} = k(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b})$ | Constants pull out |
| **Self Dot Product** | $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{a} = |\vec{a}|^2$ | Gives magnitude squared |

---

## Special Cases & Their Meaning

| Condition | $\theta$ | $\cos\theta$ | $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}$ | Interpretation |
|-----------|----------|--------------|-------------------------|----------------|
| Same direction | $0°$ | $1$ | $\|\vec{a}\|\|\vec{b}\|$ (maximum) | Vectors aligned |
| Acute angle | $0° < \theta < 90°$ | $+$ve | Positive | Mostly same direction |
| **Perpendicular** | $90°$ | $0$ | **Zero** | Vectors orthogonal |
| Obtuse angle | $90° < \theta < 180°$ | $-$ve | Negative | Mostly opposite |
| Opposite direction | $180°$ | $-1$ | $-\|\vec{a}\|\|\vec{b}\|$ (minimum) | Vectors anti-parallel |

---

## ⭐ Orthogonality Condition (Very Important!)

$$\Large \boxed{\vec{a} \perp \vec{b} \iff \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0}$$

Two non-zero vectors are **perpendicular** if and only if their dot product is **zero**.

---

## Unit Vector Dot Products

| Product | Value | Reason |
|---------|-------|--------|
| $\hat{i} \cdot \hat{i}$ | $1$ | Same direction, $\cos 0° = 1$ |
| $\hat{j} \cdot \hat{j}$ | $1$ | Same direction |
| $\hat{k} \cdot \hat{k}$ | $1$ | Same direction |
| $\hat{i} \cdot \hat{j}$ | $0$ | Perpendicular, $\cos 90° = 0$ |
| $\hat{j} \cdot \hat{k}$ | $0$ | Perpendicular |
| $\hat{k} \cdot \hat{i}$ | $0$ | Perpendicular |

---

## 📝 Worked Example 1: Finding Angle Between Vectors

**Problem:** Find the angle between $\vec{A} = \langle 1, 0, 0 \rangle$ and $\vec{B} = \langle 1, 1, 0 \rangle$

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Calculate the dot product
$$\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = (1)(1) + (0)(1) + (0)(0) = 1$$

**Step 2:** Calculate magnitudes
$$|\vec{A}| = \sqrt{1^2 + 0^2 + 0^2} = 1$$
$$|\vec{B}| = \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2 + 0^2} = \sqrt{2}$$

**Step 3:** Apply the formula
$$\cos\theta = \frac{\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B}}{|\vec{A}||\vec{B}|} = \frac{1}{(1)(\sqrt{2})} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$$

**Step 4:** Find the angle
$$\theta = \cos^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \boxed{45°}$$

---

## 📝 Worked Example 2: Testing Perpendicularity

**Problem:** Are $\vec{p} = 2\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}$ and $\vec{q} = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}$ perpendicular?

**Solution:**
$$\vec{p} \cdot \vec{q} = (2)(1) + (-3)(2) + (1)(4)$$
$$= 2 - 6 + 4 = 0$$

Since $\vec{p} \cdot \vec{q} = 0$, the vectors are **perpendicular**. ✓

---

## 🔧 Applications of Dot Product

### 1. Work Done by a Force
$$W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d} = |\vec{F}||\vec{d}|\cos\theta$$

### 2. Projection of Vector onto Another

**Scalar projection of $\vec{a}$ onto $\vec{b}$:**
$$\text{comp}_{\vec{b}}\vec{a} = \frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|}$$

**Vector projection of $\vec{a}$ onto $\vec{b}$:**
$$\text{proj}_{\vec{b}}\vec{a} = \frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|^2}\vec{b}$$

### 3. Testing Perpendicularity
If $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$, vectors are perpendicular.

---

# 2. 🔴 The Cross Product (Vector Product)

## What Is It?

The cross product multiplies two vectors to produce a **new vector** that is **perpendicular** to both original vectors.

> **Physical Intuition:** Creates a vector pointing "out of" the plane containing the original two vectors.

---

## Definition & Formula

### Geometric Definition
$$\Large \boxed{\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\sin\theta \, \hat{n}}$$

Where:
- $|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\sin\theta$ = magnitude of the result
- $\hat{n}$ = unit vector perpendicular to both $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$
- Direction of $\hat{n}$ determined by the **Right-Hand Rule**

---

## 🤚 The Right-Hand Rule

To find the direction of $\vec{a} \times \vec{b}$:

1. **Point** your fingers in the direction of $\vec{a}$ (first vector)
2. **Curl** your fingers toward $\vec{b}$ (second vector)  
3. **Your thumb** points in the direction of $\vec{a} \times \vec{b}$

```
        ↑ (thumb) = a × b
        |
    ----+--→ (fingers curl from a to b)
        |
```

---

## Calculation: Determinant Method

$$\Large \vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \end{vmatrix}$$

**Expansion:**

$$\Large \boxed{\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \hat{i}(a_2b_3 - a_3b_2) - \hat{j}(a_1b_3 - a_3b_1) + \hat{k}(a_1b_2 - a_2b_1)}$$

> **⚠️ Warning:** Notice the **negative sign** in front of the $\hat{j}$ component!

---

## Step-by-Step Determinant Calculation

**Cover-up method:**

| Component | Cover | Calculate |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| $\hat{i}$ component | Cover column 1, row 1 | $+(a_2b_3 - a_3b_2)$ |
| $\hat{j}$ component | Cover column 2, row 1 | $-(a_1b_3 - a_3b_1)$ |
| $\hat{k}$ component | Cover column 3, row 1 | $+(a_1b_2 - a_2b_1)$ |

> **💡 Memory Trick:** Signs alternate: $+, -, +$

---

## Key Properties

| Property | Formula | Important Note |
|----------|---------|----------------|
| **Anti-Commutative** | $\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = -(\vec{b} \times \vec{a})$ | **Order matters!** Swapping reverses direction |
| **Distributive** | $\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} + \vec{c}) = \vec{a} \times \vec{b} + \vec{a} \times \vec{c}$ | Can expand brackets |
| **Scalar Multiple** | $(k\vec{a}) \times \vec{b} = k(\vec{a} \times \vec{b})$ | Constants pull out |
| **Self Cross Product** | $\vec{a} \times \vec{a} = \vec{0}$ | Any vector crossed with itself = zero vector |
| **NOT Associative** | $\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) \neq (\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) \times \vec{c}$ | Parentheses matter! |

---

## ⭐ Parallel Vectors Condition

$$\Large \boxed{\vec{a} \parallel \vec{b} \iff \vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{0}}$$

Two non-zero vectors are **parallel** if and only if their cross product is the **zero vector**.

---

## Standard Basis Cross Products

### Cyclic Pattern
$$\hat{i} \rightarrow \hat{j} \rightarrow \hat{k} \rightarrow \hat{i}$$

```
        î
       ↗ ↖
      ↙   ↘
     k̂ ←—→ ĵ
```

| Going WITH cycle | Going AGAINST cycle |
|------------------|---------------------|
| $\hat{i} \times \hat{j} = +\hat{k}$ | $\hat{j} \times \hat{i} = -\hat{k}$ |
| $\hat{j} \times \hat{k} = +\hat{i}$ | $\hat{k} \times \hat{j} = -\hat{i}$ |
| $\hat{k} \times \hat{i} = +\hat{j}$ | $\hat{i} \times \hat{k} = -\hat{j}$ |

Also: $\hat{i} \times \hat{i} = \hat{j} \times \hat{j} = \hat{k} \times \hat{k} = \vec{0}$

---

## 📝 Worked Example 3: Computing Cross Product

**Problem:** Find $\vec{a} \times \vec{b}$ if $\vec{a} = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}$ and $\vec{b} = \hat{i} - \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$

**Solution:**

$$\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 & 2 \end{vmatrix}$$

**Step 1:** $\hat{i}$ component: $(3)(2) - (1)(-1) = 6 + 1 = 7$

**Step 2:** $\hat{j}$ component: $-[(2)(2) - (1)(1)] = -[4 - 1] = -3$

**Step 3:** $\hat{k}$ component: $(2)(-1) - (3)(1) = -2 - 3 = -5$

$$\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \boxed{7\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} - 5\hat{k}}$$

**Verification:** Check that result is perpendicular to both original vectors:
- $\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) = (2)(7) + (3)(-3) + (1)(-5) = 14 - 9 - 5 = 0$ ✓
- $\vec{b} \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) = (1)(7) + (-1)(-3) + (2)(-5) = 7 + 3 - 10 = 0$ ✓

---

## 🔧 Applications of Cross Product

### 1. Moment of Force (Torque)
$$\vec{M} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}$$

Where $\vec{r}$ is the position vector from the pivot point to where the force is applied.

### 2. Area of Parallelogram
$$\text{Area} = |\vec{AB} \times \vec{AD}|$$

### 3. Area of Triangle
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}|\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC}|$$

### 4. Finding Normal Vector to a Plane
If two vectors lie in a plane, their cross product gives a normal (perpendicular) vector to that plane.

---

## 📝 Worked Example 4: Area of Triangle

**Problem:** Find the area of triangle with vertices $A(1, 1, 1)$, $B(2, 3, 1)$, $C(1, 2, 3)$

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Find the edge vectors
$$\vec{AB} = B - A = (2-1)\hat{i} + (3-1)\hat{j} + (1-1)\hat{k} = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 0\hat{k}$$
$$\vec{AC} = C - A = (1-1)\hat{i} + (2-1)\hat{j} + (3-1)\hat{k} = 0\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$$

**Step 2:** Compute cross product
$$\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix}$$

$$= \hat{i}(4-0) - \hat{j}(2-0) + \hat{k}(1-0) = 4\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + \hat{k}$$

**Step 3:** Find magnitude
$$|\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC}| = \sqrt{16 + 4 + 1} = \sqrt{21}$$

**Step 4:** Calculate area
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}|\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC}| = \boxed{\frac{\sqrt{21}}{2} \text{ square units}}$$

# 3. 🟢 The Scalar Triple Product

## What Is It?

The scalar triple product combines **three vectors** to produce a **scalar**. It represents the **volume** of a 3D parallelepiped.

---

## Definition & Notation

$$\Large \boxed{\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = [\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c}]}$$

Also written as: $[\vec{a} \, \vec{b} \, \vec{c}]$ or $(\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c})$

---

## Calculation: 3×3 Determinant

$$\Large \vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = \begin{vmatrix} a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \\ c_1 & c_2 & c_3 \end{vmatrix}$$

**Expansion (along first row):**

$$= a_1\begin{vmatrix} b_2 & b_3 \\ c_2 & c_3 \end{vmatrix} - a_2\begin{vmatrix} b_1 & b_3 \\ c_1 & c_3 \end{vmatrix} + a_3\begin{vmatrix} b_1 & b_2 \\ c_1 & c_2 \end{vmatrix}$$

$$= a_1(b_2c_3 - b_3c_2) - a_2(b_1c_3 - b_3c_1) + a_3(b_1c_2 - b_2c_1)$$

---

## Key Properties

### Cyclic Permutation (Sign Preserved)
$$\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = \vec{b} \cdot (\vec{c} \times \vec{a}) = \vec{c} \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{b})$$

### Swapping Two Vectors (Sign Changes)
$$\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = -\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{c} \times \vec{b})$$

### Dot and Cross Are Interchangeable
$$\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = (\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) \cdot \vec{c}$$

---

## 🎯 Geometric Interpretations

### 1. Volume of Parallelepiped
$$\Large \boxed{V_{parallelepiped} = |\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c})|}$$

```
         /|--------/|
        / |       / |
       /  |      /  |  ← c
      |---|-----|   |
      |  /------|--/
      | /   ↗   | /   ← b
      |/   a    |/
      +---------+
```

### 2. Volume of Tetrahedron
$$\Large \boxed{V_{tetrahedron} = \frac{1}{6}|\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c})|}$$

> **Why 1/6?** A tetrahedron is 1/6 of the parallelepiped that encloses it.

---

## ⭐ Coplanarity Condition (Very Important!)

$$\Large \boxed{\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c} \text{ are coplanar} \iff \vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = 0}$$

**Physical Meaning:** If three vectors lie in the same plane, they cannot form a 3D box, so the "volume" is zero.

---

## 📝 Worked Example 5: Testing for Coplanarity

**Problem:** Do vectors $\vec{a} = \langle 1, 1, 0 \rangle$, $\vec{b} = \langle 0, 1, 1 \rangle$, and $\vec{c} = \langle 1, 2, 1 \rangle$ lie on the same plane?

**Solution:**

$$\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix}$$

**Expanding along the first row:**

$$= 1 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} - 1 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} + 0 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix}$$

$$= 1(1 \times 1 - 1 \times 2) - 1(0 \times 1 - 1 \times 1) + 0$$

$$= 1(1 - 2) - 1(0 - 1) + 0$$

$$= 1(-1) - 1(-1) = -1 + 1 = 0$$

Since the scalar triple product equals **zero**, the vectors are **coplanar**. ✓

---

## 📝 Worked Example 6: Volume of Tetrahedron

**Problem:** Find the volume of the tetrahedron with vertices:
$O(0, 0, 0)$, $A(1, 2, 3)$, $B(2, 1, 1)$, $C(3, 1, 2)$

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Find position vectors from origin $O$
$$\vec{OA} = \langle 1, 2, 3 \rangle$$
$$\vec{OB} = \langle 2, 1, 1 \rangle$$
$$\vec{OC} = \langle 3, 1, 2 \rangle$$

**Step 2:** Calculate scalar triple product

$$\vec{OA} \cdot (\vec{OB} \times \vec{OC}) = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 3 & 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix}$$

$$= 1(1 \times 2 - 1 \times 1) - 2(2 \times 2 - 1 \times 3) + 3(2 \times 1 - 1 \times 3)$$

$$= 1(2 - 1) - 2(4 - 3) + 3(2 - 3)$$

$$= 1(1) - 2(1) + 3(-1) = 1 - 2 - 3 = -4$$

**Step 3:** Find volume
$$V = \frac{1}{6}|\vec{OA} \cdot (\vec{OB} \times \vec{OC})| = \frac{1}{6}|-4| = \boxed{\frac{2}{3} \text{ cubic units}}$$

# 4. 🟡 The Vector Triple Product

## What Is It?

The vector triple product combines **three vectors** using two cross products to produce a **vector**.

---

## Definition

$$\Large \vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c})$$

---

## The BAC-CAB Rule ⭐

$$\Large \boxed{\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = \vec{b}(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{c}) - \vec{c}(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b})}$$

> **💡 Memory Trick:** "**B**AC minus **C**AB" - sounds like "**back** minus **cab**"

---

## ⚠️ Critical Warning: Order Matters!

The vector triple product is **NOT associative**:

$$\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) \neq (\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) \times \vec{c}$$

For the other grouping:
$$(\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) \times \vec{c} = (\vec{a} \cdot \vec{c})\vec{b} - (\vec{b} \cdot \vec{c})\vec{a}$$

---

## Understanding the Rule

In $\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = \vec{b}(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{c}) - \vec{c}(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b})$:

- The "**outside**" vector $\vec{a}$ **dots** with both vectors inside the parentheses
- The result is a **linear combination** of $\vec{b}$ and $\vec{c}$
- Therefore, the result lies in the **plane** of $\vec{b}$ and $\vec{c}$

---

## 📝 Worked Example 7: Using BAC-CAB Rule

**Problem:** Find $\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c})$ where:
- $\vec{a} = \hat{i} + \hat{j}$
- $\vec{b} = 2\hat{i} + \hat{k}$  
- $\vec{c} = \hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}$

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Calculate needed dot products
$$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{c} = (1)(1) + (1)(1) + (0)(1) = 2$$
$$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = (1)(2) + (1)(0) + (0)(1) = 2$$

**Step 2:** Apply BAC-CAB rule
$$\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = \vec{b}(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{c}) - \vec{c}(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b})$$
$$= (2\hat{i} + \hat{k})(2) - (\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k})(2)$$
$$= 4\hat{i} + 2\hat{k} - 2\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$$
$$= \boxed{2\hat{i} - 2\hat{j}}$$

# 5. 📊 Master Summary Table

| Product | Notation | Output | Formula | Geometric Meaning |
|---------|----------|--------|---------|-------------------|
| **Dot** | $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}$ | **Scalar** | $a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3$ | Projection, Angle, Work |
| **Cross** | $\vec{a} \times \vec{b}$ | **Vector** | 3×3 Determinant | Area, Normal, Torque |
| **Scalar Triple** | $\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c})$ | **Scalar** | 3×3 Determinant | Volume, Coplanarity |
| **Vector Triple** | $\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c})$ | **Vector** | BAC - CAB | Vector simplification |

# 6. ⚡ Quick Reference: Zero Conditions

| Condition | Test | Meaning |
|-----------|------|---------|
| $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$ | Dot product is zero | Vectors are **perpendicular** |
| $\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{0}$ | Cross product is zero vector | Vectors are **parallel** |
| $\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = 0$ | Scalar triple product is zero | Vectors are **coplanar** |

# 7. 🎯 When to Use Which Product?

| If you need to find... | Use... |
|------------------------|--------|
| Angle between two vectors | **Dot product** |
| Work done by a force | **Dot product** |
| Whether vectors are perpendicular | **Dot product** (check = 0) |
| A vector perpendicular to two others | **Cross product** |
| Area of triangle/parallelogram | **Cross product** (magnitude) |
| Torque/Moment of force | **Cross product** |
| Whether vectors are parallel | **Cross product** (check = 0) |
| Volume of parallelepiped | **Scalar triple product** |
| Volume of tetrahedron | **Scalar triple product** ÷ 6 |
| Whether vectors are coplanar | **Scalar triple product** (check = 0) |
| Simplifying complex vector expressions | **Vector triple product** (BAC-CAB) |

---

# 8. ⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Correct Understanding |
|---------|----------------------|
| Thinking dot product gives a vector | Dot product **always** gives a **scalar** |
| Forgetting the negative on $\hat{j}$ in cross product | Pattern is $+\hat{i}, -\hat{j}, +\hat{k}$ |
| Ignoring order in cross product | $\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = -\vec{b} \times \vec{a}$ (order matters!) |
| Forgetting $\frac{1}{2}$ for triangle area | Triangle = $\frac{1}{2}$ × parallelogram |
| Forgetting $\frac{1}{6}$ for tetrahedron | Tetrahedron = $\frac{1}{6}$ × parallelepiped |
| Thinking triple product is associative | Parentheses **matter** in $\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c})$ |
| Forgetting absolute value for volume | Volume is always **positive** |

# 9. 🔗 Important Identities Summary

### Lagrange's Identity
$$|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{a}|^2|\vec{b}|^2 - (\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b})^2$$

### BAC-CAB Rule
$$\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = \vec{b}(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{c}) - \vec{c}(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b})$$

### Scalar Triple Product Symmetry
$$\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = \vec{b} \cdot (\vec{c} \times \vec{a}) = \vec{c} \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{b})$$
